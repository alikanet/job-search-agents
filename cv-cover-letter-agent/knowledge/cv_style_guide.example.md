This file defines the rules for generating a tailored CV for a specific role. It works alongside the Cover Letter Style Guide.

## Core principle: nothing in the CV's content is static

No part of the CV's content (title, Professional Summary, Core Competencies, Tools & Platforms selection, or the wording of any experience bullet) is reused verbatim across applications. Each is reworded and fine-tuned for the specific job spec, every time.

The only constants are objective facts: name, phone, email, location line, any qualification line, and the standard section headings (fixed for ATS parsing). The facts in the Career History never change. The wording almost always should.

## Priority: ATS and AI screening

The goal is to clear keyword-based applicant tracking systems (ATS) and newer AI screening layers, as far as format and content genuinely allow.

**Mandatory keyword mirroring step, before drafting Core Competencies or bullets:**
1. Extract every specific skill, tool, methodology, certification and role-title term from the job spec.
2. Check each term against the Career History. Mark it as a genuine match, a rephrasing opportunity, or not present.
3. For genuine matches and rephrasing opportunities, use the job spec's own terminology wherever it is a truthful fit.
4. For terms with no real basis, do not use them. Never invent a skill or keyword to match a posting.

**Out of scope:** employer-set rules such as years of experience, location or right to work. No CV structure can guarantee clearing these. Never claim otherwise.

## Formatting rules (machine readability)

- Single column layout only. No tables, text boxes, headers, footers or images.
- Use these exact section headings, unchanged: PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY, CORE COMPETENCIES, PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE, PASSION PROJECT, EDUCATION & QUALIFICATIONS, TOOLS & PLATFORMS.
- Reverse chronological order.
- Dates written consistently as Month YYYY – Month YYYY.
- Real job titles from the Career History are used verbatim. Only the headline and summary framing can be reframed per role.
- Spell out acronyms at least once.
- Plain round bullets only. No unusual glyphs or emoji.
- Contact details sit as plain text at the top of the document body, never in a header or footer.
- Name written in standard title case, never all capitals.
- Output as a Word document (.docx).
- No markdown syntax as literal characters in the output.

## Tone and voice

**REVIEW: these are default choices. Replace them with rules that match how you want to sound. Use the voice and style prompt if you are unsure.**

- Confident, plain-spoken, first person. State things as fact backed by real, quantified achievements.
- No hedging language ("I believe", "I feel", "I think I could").
- No false modesty and no unsupported bravado.
- Natural and direct, the way you would say it out loud in a conversation, not stiff corporate prose.
- Spelling: **[BRITISH or AMERICAN]**.
- The tone applies most strongly to the Professional Summary. Core Competencies and bullets stay factual and quantified.

## Sentence style

**REVIEW:**

- Mix short declarative sentences and the occasional deliberate fragment with longer explanatory sentences.
- Direct address to the reader ("you") is fine in the Professional Summary, if you want it.

## Punctuation

**REVIEW:**

- Use hyphens normally in compound words (multi-site, end-to-end).
- Do not use em dashes or en dashes as a stylistic device for asides or dramatic pauses. Use commas, full stops or separate sentences.
- Straight quotation marks and apostrophes only. Write an ellipsis as three full stops.

## Professional Summary: tailored per role

Rewritten for every application. Cover as many of the job spec's distinct requirements as your real experience honestly allows. Single paragraph, never split. Shape: a positioning statement, then the proof points that most directly answer the spec, then a differentiator or closing line. Never invent a skill or outcome. If a requirement cannot be supported, leave it out or ask the user.

## Experience bullet rules

Every bullet follows one formula: what you did, how you did it, and the result. Use a measurable result wherever the Career History records one. Never alter or invent a figure. If the how or the result is missing from the Career History, ask the user rather than guessing.
